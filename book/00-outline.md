# Indonesian Affixation: A Study Guide — Outline

Status: all 13 chapters and all five appendices are drafted. Scope: affix
system only (not a full grammar/vocabulary course). See Appendix C for a
disclosure of how this draft was produced (AI-assisted) and its known
reliability caveats, before relying on specific claims.

1. Introduction — root words (*kata dasar*), word classes, what affixation
   does to meaning and voice, notation conventions used throughout the book.
   [DRAFTED — see 01-introduction.md]
2. Phonology You Need First — nasal assimilation for *meN-*/*peN-*; the
   r-initial dissimilation rule shared by *ber-*/*ter-*/*per-*
   (→ *be-*/*te-*/*pe-*); syllable basics needed to make sense of the rules.
   [DRAFTED — see 02-phonology.md]
3. Active Voice: *me-* — the base active prefix, actor focus, when it's
   required vs. optional. [DRAFTED — see 03-active-me.md]
4. Active Voice, Extended: *me-...-kan* and *me-...-i* — causative vs.
   locative/repetitive readings, and how to tell them apart on a given root.
   [DRAFTED — see 04-active-extended.md]
5. Passive Voice: *di-* and *ter-* — the *me-*/*di-* mirror pair, and *ter-*
   as accidental/stative passive. [DRAFTED — see 05-passive-di-ter.md]
6. Intransitive *ber-* — "to have/use/be in a state of," plus the
   *ber-...-an* reciprocal/plural circumfix.
   [DRAFTED — see 06-intransitive-ber.md]
7. Bare *-kan* and *-i* — suffix-only imperative/colloquial forms that drop
   *me-*, and when that's natural vs. marked. [DRAFTED — see
   07-bare-kan-i.md]
8. Nominalization I: Agent & Instrument — *pe-* — "one who does" / "thing
   used to," shares nasal assimilation with *me-*. [DRAFTED — see
   08-nominal-pe.md]
9. Nominalization II: Abstract & Result Nouns — *-an*, *ke-...-an*,
   *pe-...-an*, *per-...-an* — four nominalizing patterns and how to tell
   which applies (including the lexically-arbitrary *pe-...-an* vs
   *per-...-an* split). [DRAFTED — see 09-nominal-abstract.md]
10. Modifiers & Particles — *ke-*, *se-*, *-nya*, *-lah* — ordinals,
    "one/same/as...as," definiteness/nominalizer, and clitic-like particles
    (*-kah*, *-pun*, *-tah*). [DRAFTED — see 10-modifiers-particles.md]
11. Infixes — *-el-*, *-er-*, *-em-* — the small closed, mostly-fossilized
    set (*gelembung*, *gemuruh*, *telunjuk*). [DRAFTED — see 11-infixes.md]
12. Irregulars & Lexical Idiosyncrasies — a catalog of roots that defy the
    general rules (meaning drift, blocked combinations, fossilized forms).
    [DRAFTED — see 12-irregulars.md]
13. Synthesis: Parsing a Word — worked examples stripping a real word down
    to root + affixes, plus practice sentences. [DRAFTED — see
    13-synthesis.md]

register equivalent," and "already covered by a native affix you
learned for a different reason." [DRAFTED — see
18-appendix-e-english-affix-gaps.md]
