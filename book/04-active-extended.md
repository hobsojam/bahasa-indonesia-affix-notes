# Chapter 4: Active Voice, Extended — *me-...-kan* and *me-...-i*

## 4.1 Overview

Chapter 3 covered bare *me-*. Many roots also combine *me-* with a
second suffix, *-kan* or *-i*, to add a causative or
locative/repetitive reading on top of the basic active voice. These
aren't optional flourishes: for a lot of roots, the bare *me-* form
either doesn't exist or means something different, and *-kan*/*-i* is
what actually carries the specific meaning you want.

| Circumfix | Core reading | Typical object |
|---|---|---|
| *me-...-kan* | causative ("make X happen/be") or benefactive ("do X for someone") | the thing caused, created, or given |
| *me-...-i* | locative ("do X to/on a place or person") or repeated/distributed action | the place, person, or target of repeated action |

## 4.2 Forms

Both suffixes attach after the same nasal-assimilated *meN-* prefix
from Chapter 3 (§3.2) — the nasal assimilation rule doesn't change at
all when a suffix is added:

| Root | *meN-* alone | + *-kan* | + *-i* |
|---|---|---|---|
| *tulis* (write) | menulis | menuliskan | menulisi |
| *dekat* (near) | mendekat | mendekatkan | mendekati |
| *jauh* (far) | menjauh | menjauhkan | menjauhi |
| *temu* (meet, encounter) | — | menemukan | menemui |

(*temu* illustrates the *t*-drop rule from §3.2 carrying through into
the circumfix: *te-* + *-mu* loses its *t* to the nasal the same way
*tulis* does, giving *men-emukan*, not *mentemukan*.)

## 4.3 Usage notes

### 4.3.1 *-kan*: causative

*-kan* turns "do X" into "cause X to happen/exist," with the caused
thing as the object:

> *dekat* ("near") → **mendekatkan**: Dia **mendekatkan** kursi ke meja.
> "He **brought** the chair **closer** to the table."

> *jauh* ("far") → **menjauhkan**: Simpan racun itu dan **jauhkan** dari
> anak-anak.
> "Keep that poison and **keep** it **away** from children."

> *temu* ("meet, find") → **menemukan**: Saya **menemukan** kunci saya.
> "I **found** my key." (I caused the key to go from "not found" to
> "found" — the object is the thing found, not a person.)

### 4.3.2 *-kan*: benefactive

A second, related reading of *-kan*: doing the action *for* someone,
with that beneficiary promoted to object (and the thing acted on
demoted to a second, *oleh*-less complement):

> *tulis* ("write") → **menuliskan**: Dia **menuliskan** saya surat.
> "He **wrote** me a letter." (wrote a letter, for my benefit)

### 4.3.3 *-i*: locative

*-i* puts the place, surface, or target of the action in object
position, rather than the thing created or moved:

> *tulis* ("write") → **menulisi**: Dia **menulisi** dinding itu.
> "He **wrote on** that wall." (the wall is the object — what got
> written *on*, not what got written)

> *dekat* ("near") → **mendekati**: Kucing itu **mendekati** saya.
> "That cat **approached** me." (the thing being neared is the object)

### 4.3.4 *-i*: repeated or distributed action

A related sense of *-i*: the action happens repeatedly, or spread
across the object, rather than once:

> *jauh* ("far") → **menjauhi**: Saya **menjauhi** orang itu.
> "I **avoid**/**stay away from** that person." (an ongoing,
> repeated avoidance, not a single instance of distancing)

### 4.3.5 Minimal pairs on the same root: how to tell *-kan* and *-i* apart

The clearest way to internalize the *-kan*/*-i* split is to hold the
root fixed and compare:

| Root | *-kan* form | Gloss | *-i* form | Gloss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *ajar* (teach/learn) | **mengajarkan** | "to teach *something* (to someone)" — object = the thing taught | **mengajari** | "to teach *someone*" — object = the person taught |
| *dekat* (near) | **mendekatkan** | "to bring something closer" — object = thing moved | **mendekati** | "to approach something/someone" — object = target neared |
| *jauh* (far) | **menjauhkan** | "to move something away, keep apart" — object = thing moved | **menjauhi** | "to avoid, stay away from" — object = thing avoided |
| *temu* (meet/find) | **menemukan** | "to find, discover" — object = thing found | **menemui** | "to go meet, visit" — object = person visited |

A useful heuristic (not an exceptionless rule — check real examples
when unsure): if the object is a **person being affected or visited**,
*-i* is likelier; if the object is a **thing being produced, moved, or
caused into a state**, *-kan* is likelier. *mengajar(i/kan)* is the
classic example precisely because both objects are possible on the same
root and the suffix is what tells you which one you're looking at.

## 4.4 Trap: the bare root doesn't always predict which suffix exists

Not every root takes both *-kan* and *-i* naturally — which one(s) a
given root actually uses is lexically specific, the same way Chapter
3 flagged that some roots need an extension at all (§3.3.3). From
Chapter 3's own example:

> *naik* (to go up) + *-kan* → **menaikkan** ✓ "to raise, increase,
> promote"
> *naik* + *-i* → **menaiki** ✓ "to ride, to board"

Both exist here, with clearly different meanings — but don't assume
that's guaranteed for every root. Check a dictionary (or
`pembilas/data/annotations.json`, as a secondary corroboration) rather
than assuming symmetry.

## 4.5 Trap: *-kan* and *-i* can both exist on a root with unrelated
meanings

Beyond the "which suffix does what job" question, watch for roots
where one of the two forms has drifted into an idiomatic or figurative
sense that doesn't follow cleanly from the general pattern. For
example *kerja* ("work") has an attested, common *-i* form, **mengerjai**,
whose everyday meaning is "to prank/trick someone" — not a literal
locative "to work on someone." Treat that as a lexical fact about
*kerja* specifically, not as a second general function of *-i*.

## 4.6 A related pattern: *memper-*

A third prefix, *memper-* (*meN-* fused with *per-*), forms a further
family of causative-leaning verbs, mostly on **adjective** roots. It's
grouped here with *-kan*/*-i* because it's built the same way — an
active-voice *meN-* layer stacked with another morpheme — but it's
enough of its own thing, with enough disagreement even among linguists
about how exactly to characterize it, that it's worth treating as a
"related pattern to be aware of" rather than folding it into the
*-kan*/*-i* system above.

**On an adjective root, with no further suffix**, *memper-* means "to
make more [adjective]":

> *besar* (big) → **memperbesar**: Dia **memperbesar** foto itu.
> "He **enlarged**/**made bigger** that photo."
> *cepat* (fast) → **mempercepat**: Mereka **mempercepat** proses itu.
> "They **sped up** that process."
> *lambat* (slow) → **memperlambat**: Obat ini **memperlambat**
> penyembuhan.
> "This medicine **slows down** healing."

**Trap: don't add *-kan* on top of *memper-* on these adjective
roots** — *memperbesarkan* is a commonly cited error, not a stronger
or more formal version of *memperbesar*. (A separately valid word,
**membesarkan**, exists on the same root via the ordinary *meN-...-kan*
causative from Chapter 4 alone, with no *per-* involved — don't
conflate the two derivations just because they look similar.)

*memper-* also combines with *-kan* or *-i* directly, on other
(often noun or verb) roots, with results closer to an ordinary
causative:

> *tahan* (hold, endure) → **mempertahankan**: "to defend, uphold,
> maintain"
> *dagang* (trade) → **memperdagangkan**: "to trade, deal in"
> *baik* (good) → **memperbaiki**: "to fix, repair, improve"

Published grammatical analyses don't fully agree on whether to call
this *-kan*/*-i* combination a causative or an intensifier, and which
roots take which variant isn't predictable from a simple rule — treat
*memper-* overall as a pattern to recognize when you meet it, check
per word in a dictionary, and expect more lexical idiosyncrasy here
than in the *-kan*/*-i* system covered earlier in this chapter.

## 4.7 Quick reference

| | *meN-* (Ch. 3) | *meN-...-kan* | *meN-...-i* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adds | nothing beyond active voice | causative / benefactive | locative / repetitive |
| Typical object | the plain undergoer, if transitive | the thing caused/created/given | the place/person targeted |
