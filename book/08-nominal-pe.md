# Chapter 8: Nominalization I — Agent & Instrument *pe-*

## 8.1 Overview

*pe-* turns a verb (occasionally adjective) root into a noun naming
**the one who does the action** (agent) or **the thing used to do it**
(instrument). It's the nominal counterpart to *me-*: wherever *me-*
names an action, *pe-* on the same root tends to name the doer or tool
of that action.

| Root | *me-* form (Ch. 3) | *pe-* form | Gloss |
|---|---|---|---|
| *tulis* (write) | menulis | **penulis** | "writer" |
| *curi* (steal) | mencuri | **pencuri** | "thief" |
| *potong* (cut) | memotong | **pemotong** | "cutter" (person *or* tool) |

## 8.2 Forms: shares nasal assimilation with *me-*

*pe-* undergoes exactly the same nasal assimilation as *me-*
(§2.2) — substitute *pe-* for *me-* in every row of that table:

| Root starts with | *peN-* surfaces as | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *b, f, v* | *pem-* | *bantu* → **pembantu** ("helper, assistant") |
| *p* | *pem-* (p dropped) | *potong* → **pemotong** ("cutter") |
| *c, j, sy, z* | *pen-* | *curi* → **pencuri** ("thief") |
| *d* | *pen-* | *dengar* → **pendengar** ("listener") |
| *t* | *pen-* (t dropped) | *tulis* → **penulis** ("writer") |
| *g, h, kh* | *peng-* | *gambar* → **penggambar** ("drafter, drawer") |
| *k* | *peng-* (k dropped) | *kirim* → **pengirim** ("sender") |
| vowel | *peng-* | *ambil* → **pengambil** ("the one who takes") |
| *s* | *peny-* (s dropped) | *sapu* → **penyapu** ("sweeper") |
| *l, m, n, r, w, y, ng, ny* | *pe-* (no nasal inserted) | *lari* → **pelari** ("runner") |

## 8.3 Usage notes

### 8.3.1 Agent: "one who does X"

> Dia seorang **penulis**.
> "She is a **writer**."

> Polisi menangkap **pencuri** itu.
> "The police caught that **thief**."

### 8.3.2 Instrument: "thing used to do X"

> **Pemotong** rumput itu rusak.
> "That lawn **mower** (grass cutter) is broken."

### 8.3.3 Trap: *pe-* alone doesn't tell you agent vs. instrument

The same *pe-* form is often ambiguous between a person and a tool,
and the two readings are told apart by context (or by a following noun
that narrows it into a compound), not by any difference in the affix
itself:

> **pemotong** — could mean "a person who cuts" or "a cutting tool,"
> depending on context.
> **pemotong rumput** — specifically "a *lawnmower*/grass cutter"
> (instrument reading pinned down by the compound).
> **pemotong daging** — could be "a *butcher*" (agent) or "a meat
> slicer" (instrument) without more context.

### 8.3.4 "One who is X" on adjective roots

*pe-* also attaches to some adjective roots to mean "a person
characterized by X":

> *malas* (lazy) → **pemalas**: Dia **pemalas**.
> "He's **a lazy person**."

This use is lexically limited — it doesn't apply to every adjective
root, so don't assume a *pe-* form exists for a given adjective without
checking.

## 8.4 Trap: some roots take *pe-* irregularly, without the expected
nasal change

A small number of roots take bare *pe-* instead of the nasal-assimilated
form §8.2 would predict. The clearest case: *kerja* ("work") gives
**pekerja** ("worker"), not the regular *k*-drop form *pengerja* that
§8.2's table would otherwise produce. This is the same lexical
irregularity already flagged for *kerja* under *ber-* in §6.2 — it isn't
a second phonological rule, just a fact about this specific root,
cataloged further in Chapter 12.

## Practice

Give the *pe-* form for each root (name which row of the §8.2 table
applies), and flag whether it's the agent or instrument reading — or
both, if context alone decides:

1. *ajar* (teach)
2. *latih* (train, coach)
3. *lari* (run)

<details>
<summary>Answer key</summary>

1. **pengajar** — vowel-initial → *peng-*, root kept intact. Agent:
   "teacher, instructor."
2. **pelatih** — *l*-initial → *pe-*, no nasal insertion. Agent: "coach,
   trainer."
3. **pelari** — *l*-initial → *pe-*, no nasal insertion. Agent:
   "runner" (can also mean "fugitive," depending on context).

</details>
