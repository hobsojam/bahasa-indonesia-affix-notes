# Chapter 6: Intransitive *ber-*

## 6.1 Overview

*ber-* is the main prefix for forming **intransitive** verbs — verbs
with no direct object. It covers a cluster of related readings: "to
have/wear/use X," "to be in a state of X," and the plain intransitive
counterpart of a transitive *meN-* verb on the same root. A reciprocal/
plural circumfix, *ber-...-an*, is built on the same prefix and is
covered at the end of this chapter.

| Attaches to | Core reading | Example |
|---|---|---|
| noun root | "to have/wear/use X" | *baju* ("shirt") → **berbaju** ("to wear a shirt") |
| verb root | intransitive counterpart of a *meN-* verb | *jalan* ("road"/"walk") → **berjalan** ("to walk") |
| adjective/stative root | "to be/become X" | *bahagia* ("happy") → **berbahagia** ("to be happy") |

## 6.2 Forms

*ber-* attaches as-is to most roots. Two sound changes apply:

**r-initial dissimilation** (§2.3): *ber-* drops its own *r* before a
root that itself starts with *r*:

> *rasa* (feel) → **berasa** (not *berrasa*) — "to feel like, taste
> of"

**Lexical exceptions**: a small, closed set of roots trigger the same
*ber-*→*be-* reduction even though they don't start with *r* —
*kerja* → **bekerja** (not *berkerja*), *ajar* → **belajar** (not
*berajar*). These don't follow from the *r*-initial rule (neither root
starts with *r*) and aren't predictable from any other rule given in
this book; they're memorized exceptions, cataloged together with other
irregulars in Chapter 12. Don't extend them into a general pattern —
*cermin* ("mirror"), for instance, takes the fully regular
**bercermin** ("to look in the mirror"), not *becermin*.

## 6.3 Usage notes

### 6.3.1 "To have/wear/use X" (noun roots)

> Dia **berbaju** merah.
> "She **is wearing** a red shirt."

> Rumah itu **berpagar** tinggi.
> "That house **has** a tall **fence**."

### 6.3.2 Intransitive counterpart of a *meN-* verb

Many roots have both a transitive *meN-* form (Chapter 3) and an
intransitive *ber-* form, and the two are not interchangeable — the
*ber-* form can't take a direct object the way the *meN-* form can:

> *jalan* → **berjalan** ("to walk," intransitive): Dia **berjalan** ke
> sekolah. "She **walks** to school." (no object)

> Compare the transitive *meN-* pattern from Chapter 3 — *jalan* with
> *-kan* (Chapter 4) gives **menjalankan**, "to run/operate
> [something]": Dia **menjalankan** mesin itu. "He **operates** that
> machine." (object = the machine)

> *kerja* → **bekerja** ("to work," intransitive, irregular *be-* form
> per §6.2): Saya **bekerja** di sana. "I **work** there." (no object)

> *hasil* → **berhasil** ("to succeed, to turn out successfully"):
> Usaha itu **berhasil**. "That effort **succeeded**."

> *temu* → **bertemu** ("to meet," reciprocal-leaning intransitive —
> see §6.3.4): Kami **bertemu** di kafe. "We **met** at the café."

### 6.3.3 "To be/become X" (adjective/stative roots)

On an adjective root, *ber-* often adds a sense of actively being in,
or entering, that state, rather than simply describing it the way the
bare adjective does:

> *bahagia* (happy, adjective) → **berbahagia**: Semoga Anda
> **berbahagia** selalu. "May you **always be happy**."

This use is less universal than the noun/verb-root patterns above —
many adjectives are simply used bare as predicates (*Dia bahagia*, "She
is happy," with no *ber-* needed) and don't require or even commonly
take a *ber-* form. Check a dictionary before assuming an adjective
root has a natural *ber-* form; don't manufacture one from a rule.

### 6.3.4 *ber-...-an*: reciprocal or plural/distributed action

Adding *-an* on top of *ber-* forms a circumfix with two related
readings, both built on the idea of an action involving more than one
participant or instance at once:

**(a) Reciprocal** ("X each other"):

> *kenal* (to know, be acquainted) → **berkenalan**: Kami **berkenalan**
> di pesta itu.
> "We **got acquainted (with each other)** at that party."

> *salam* (greeting) → **bersalaman**: Mereka **bersalaman** sebelum
> rapat.
> "They **shook hands (with each other)** before the meeting."

**(b) Plural/distributed action** ("X one after another, scattered,
many at once"):

> *datang* (to come) → **berdatangan**: Tamu-tamu **berdatangan** sejak
> pagi.
> "Guests **kept arriving** (one after another) since morning."

> *jatuh* (to fall) → **berjatuhan**: Daun-daun **berjatuhan** dari
> pohon itu.
> "Leaves **fell** (one by one, scattered) from that tree."

Contrast plain *bertemu* (§6.3.2, a simple reciprocal "to meet" already
built into the root pairing) against *ber-...-an* added to a root that
doesn't already carry that reciprocal sense on its own — *kenal* means
just "to know/recognize" bare, and it's the *-an* that adds "each
other."

## 6.4 Trap: *ber-* vs. the *meN-* form on the same root isn't always a
simple transitive/intransitive split

For some roots the *ber-* and *meN-* forms diverge further than "same
meaning, minus/plus an object" — *usaha* ("effort") gives **berusaha**
("to try, make an effort," the expected intransitive) but its
*meN-...-kan* form, **mengusahakan**, means "to arrange for, to make
happen on someone's behalf" rather than a simple transitive version of
"to try." Treat the transitive/intransitive pairing as a strong
tendency, not a guarantee that the meanings line up one-to-one.

## 6.5 Register note: bare root instead of *ber-* in casual speech

*ber-* gets the same casual-register treatment already flagged for
*meN-* in §3.3.2: in relaxed spoken Indonesian, it's common to drop
*ber-* and use the bare root where formal/written Indonesian would
keep the prefix:

> (formal) Saya **bekerja** di Jakarta. / (casual) Saya **kerja** di
> Jakarta.
> "I work in Jakarta."

> (formal) Dia **berjalan** ke sekolah. / (casual) Dia **jalan** ke
> sekolah.
> "She walks to school."

As with bare *meN-* (§3.3.2) and bare *-kan*/*-i* (Chapter 7), this is
purely a register choice — the bare root doesn't change what the
sentence means, only how formal it sounds. Formal writing keeps *ber-*
in a declarative statement; it's only safe to drop in casual speech,
not in writing you'd want to look polished.
