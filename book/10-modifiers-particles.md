# Chapter 10: Modifiers & Particles

## 10.1 Overview

This chapter gathers four affixes and three clitic-like particles that
don't fit neatly into the voice/nominalization system covered so far —
they modify number, equivalence, definiteness, or the illocutionary
force (command/question/emphasis) of a sentence instead.

| Form | Core job |
|---|---|
| *ke-* | ordinal numbers; a few fossilized nominals |
| *se-* | "one (whole) X"; "the same X"; "as X as" |
| *-nya* | 3rd-person possessive/definiteness marker; nominalizer |
| *-lah* | softens imperatives; adds emphasis |
| *-kah* | turns a statement into a yes/no question (formal) |
| *-pun* | "also, even"; combines with question words for "-ever" |
| *-tah* | archaic literary question particle (rare) |

*ke-* and *se-* are prefixes; *-nya*, *-lah*, *-kah*, *-pun*, and
*-tah* are suffixes/enclitic particles. *-kah*, *-pun*, and *-tah* fall
outside the 18-affix set this book otherwise cross-checks against
`pembilas/data/affixes.json` (that file doesn't model particles at
all), so this section's three particle entries are sourced directly
from standard grammar references rather than from that file.

## 10.2 *ke-*: ordinal numbers

*ke-* on a number root forms an ordinal:

> *dua* (two) → **kedua**: "second"
> *tiga* (three) → **ketiga**: "third"
> *empat* (four) → **keempat**: "fourth"

### Trap: "first" is irregular

*satu* ("one") doesn't productively give *kesatu* as the everyday word
for "first" — **pertama** is the normal word, built on a different
root/pattern entirely (not *ke-* + *satu*). *kesatu* does exist and
is attested, but mainly in formal or list contexts (e.g. numbered legal
clauses); default to *pertama* unless you have a specific reason to
use the other.

### A few fossilized *ke-* nominals

A handful of nouns look like *ke-* + root but are lexicalized,
non-productive forms rather than live instances of the ordinal
pattern — e.g. *ketua* ("chairperson, leader," built on *tua* "old")
and *kekasih* ("sweetheart, beloved," built on *kasih* "love,
affection"). Don't use these as a model for deriving new *ke-* nouns;
they're catalogued as idiosyncratic forms in Chapter 12, not examples
of a general rule.

## 10.3 *se-*: "one," "same," "as...as"

**"A whole/single X":**

> *kamar* (room) → **sekamar**: Dia teman **sekamar** saya.
> "He is my **roommate**." (lit. "same-room friend")

**"The same X":**

> *kota* (city) → **sekota**: Kami tinggal **sekota**.
> "We live **in the same city**."

**Equative comparison, "as X as":**

> *tinggi* (tall) → **setinggi**: Dia **setinggi** kakaknya.
> "He is **as tall as** his older sibling."
> *cantik* (pretty) → **secantik**: ... **secantik** bulan.
> "... **as pretty as** the moon."
> *jauh* (far) → **sejauh**: **Sejauh ini**, semuanya baik-baik saja.
> "**So far**, everything is fine." (idiomatic use of the equative
> pattern)

### Trap: not every word that looks like *se-* + root actually is

*sekolah* ("school") looks exactly like *se-* + a root *kolah*, but
*kolah* isn't an independent Indonesian root with its own meaning —
*sekolah* is a borrowing (via Malay, ultimately from Portuguese
*escola*) that happens to be spelled as if it decomposed this way.
Don't use it as a *se-* example; it's a coincidental resemblance, not
a real instance of the pattern.

## 10.4 *-nya*: possessive/definiteness marker and nominalizer

*-nya* has several distinct jobs, all built around referring back to
something already established:

**(a) Third-person possessive clitic** ("his/her/its"):

> *rumah* (house) → **rumahnya**: "his/her **house**"

**(b) Definiteness marker** ("the," when there's no explicit
possessor):

> **Bukunya** di atas meja.
> "**The book** is on the table." (not "his/her book," if context makes
> clear no one owns it in particular)

**(c) Nominalizer**, turning an adjective or verb into an abstract
reference to its quality or extent — "the X-ness of it," "how X it
is":

> *besar* (big) → **besarnya**: **Besarnya** rumah itu mengejutkan
> saya.
> "**The size** of that house surprised me." (lit. "its bigness")
> *jauh* (far) → **jauhnya**: Saya tidak tahu **jauhnya** dari sini.
> "I don't know **how far** it is from here."

**(d) Lexicalized adverbs** built on *-nya*, best learned as whole
words rather than derived fresh each time:

> *akhir* (end) → **akhirnya**: "**finally**, in the end"
> *biasa* (usual) → **biasanya**: "**usually**"
> *se-* + *benar* (true) + *-nya* → **sebenarnya**: "**actually, in
> fact**"

This last group is idiomatic rather than fully predictable from (a)–(c)
— treat them as vocabulary to learn directly.

## 10.5 *-lah*: softened imperative and emphasis

**Softening a command** (closer to "please," or just taking the edge
off a bare imperative):

> *pergi* (go) → **Pergilah!**: "**Go on**, then!" / "Please go."
> *tolong* (help) → **Tolonglah!**: "**Please** help!"

**Emphatic assertion/identification:**

> **Dialah** orangnya.
> "**He's** the one." (emphatic *dia*, "he/she," + *-lah*)

## 10.6 *-kah*: formal yes/no question marker

*-kah* attaches to the element being questioned, typically fronted,
marking a formal yes/no question:

> *apa* (what) → **Apakah** kamu lelah?
> "**Are** you tired?" (lit. "what-question you tired")
> *benar* (true) → **Benarkah**?
> "**Is that true**? / Really?"

In casual speech, intonation alone usually does this job and *-kah* is
dropped; it's mainly a formal/written-register marker.

## 10.7 *-pun*: "also/even," and "-ever" with question words

**Concessive/additive** ("even," "also," conceding a point):

> Dia **pun** setuju.
> "**Even he** agreed." / "He, too, agreed."

**Combined with a question word, "-ever":**

> *apa* (what) + *pun* → **apa pun**: "**whatever**"
> *siapa* (who) + *pun* → **siapa pun**: "**whoever**"
> *kapan* (when) + *pun* → **kapan pun**: "**whenever**"

## 10.8 *-tah*: a rare question particle

*-tah* is grouped by linguists alongside *-lah* and *-kah* as a
discourse/question particle historically found in Malay, but it has
essentially no presence in standard modern Indonesian — the available
documentation of it as a living form points to scattered regional or
colloquial Malay varieties, not standard Indonesian usage, literary or
otherwise. Treat it as effectively inert for your purposes: included
here only for completeness of the particle set this chapter's outline
entry calls out, not because you should expect to produce or encounter
it in ordinary modern Indonesian. (Note: the common modern word
*entahlah*, "who knows," is *entah* + *-lah*, an unrelated word — not
an instance of *-tah*.)

## Practice

1. Give the ordinal for *lima* (five), and name the irregular ordinal
   word for "first."
2. Form the equative ("as X as") *se-* word for *cantik* (pretty), and
   gloss it.
3. Identify which job *-nya* is doing in: **Tingginya** gedung itu
   luar biasa. ("The height of that building is extraordinary.")
