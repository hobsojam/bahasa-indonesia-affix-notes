# Chapter 5: Passive Voice — *di-* and *ter-*

## 5.1 Overview

Indonesian has two passive-like prefixes that look similar from a distance
but do different jobs:

| Prefix | Core function | Agent involvement |
|---|---|---|
| *di-* | Deliberate passive — "X is/was done (by someone)" | Agent present, usually statable |
| *ter-* | Accidental passive, resulting state, or potential | Agent absent, unknown, or irrelevant |

*di-* is the direct mirror of *me-*: whatever *me-* can do actively, *di-*
can do passively, with the same root.

```
menulis  (to write, active)   ↔   ditulis  (to be written, passive)
membaca  (to read, active)    ↔   dibaca   (to be read, passive)
```

## 5.2 Forming *di-*

Unlike *me-*, **_di-_ never changes shape.** It attaches as-is to any root,
with no nasal assimilation:

| Root | *me-* form (assimilates) | *di-* form (invariant) |
|---|---|---|
| *tulis* (write) | *menulis* | *ditulis* |
| *pukul* (hit) | *memukul* | *dipukul* |
| *kirim* (send) | *mengirim* | *dikirim* |

This is a useful diagnostic: if you see a prefix *di-* with no consonant
change at the boundary, that's consistent with it being this passive
prefix rather than some other process.

## 5.3 Expressing the agent

With a full noun phrase as agent, use *oleh* ("by"):

> Surat itu ditulis **oleh** adik saya.
> "The letter was written **by** my younger sibling."

*oleh* can be dropped when the agent is a short pronoun directly after the
verb:

> Surat itu ditulis**nya**.
> "The letter was written **by him/her**." (lit. "written-his/her")

**A trap for learners coming from English passives:** when the agent is a
first- or second-person pronoun (*saya*, *kamu*, *kita*...), Indonesian
usually prefers a different construction entirely — the pronoun moves in
front of the bare root, and *di-* disappears:

> Buku itu **saya** baca. (preferred)
> *not* Buku itu **dibaca saya**. (unnatural)
> "I read that book." / lit. "That book, I read."

This alternate construction (sometimes called Passive Type 1, vs. *di-*'s
Passive Type 2) is its own topic — flagging it here so *di-* + 1st/2nd
person pronoun doesn't surprise you later; it isn't covered in depth in
this chapter.

## 5.4 *di-* with the causative/locative extensions

*di-* stacks with the same *-kan* and *-i* suffixes covered for *me-* in
Chapter 4, carrying the same causative / locative-repetitive distinction
into the passive:

| Active | Passive | Gloss |
|---|---|---|
| *menuliskan* | *dituliskan* | "to write [something] for someone" → "was written for someone" |
| *menulisi* | *ditulisi* | "to write on [something]" → "was written on" |

## 5.5 *ter-*: accidental and stative passive

*ter-* also forms a passive-like meaning, but **without implying a
deliberate agent.** It covers two related readings:

**(a) Accidental / unintentional action:**

> Dia **tertidur** di kelas.
> "He **fell asleep** (unintentionally / dozed off) in class."
> (contrast *tidur*, the neutral verb "to sleep")

> Saya **terjatuh** di tangga.
> "I **fell** (accidentally) on the stairs."

**(b) Resulting state** — often the most natural English translation is an
adjective, but *ter-* is still grammatically a verbal/passive prefix:

> Pintu itu **terbuka**.
> "The door is/was **open**." (state resulting from an opening event)

> Suratnya **tertulis** dengan rapi.
> "The letter **is written** neatly."

Compare state (*ter-*) against deliberate action (*di-*) on the same root:

| Root | *ter-* (state/accident) | *di-* (deliberate action) |
|---|---|---|
| *buka* (open) | *terbuka* — "open" (is in an open state) | *dibuka* — "was opened" (by someone, as an event) |
| *tutup* (close) | *tertutup* — "closed" (is in a closed state) | *ditutup* — "was closed" (by someone, as an event) |
| *angkat* (lift) | *terangkat* — "got lifted" / ended up lifted | *diangkat* — "was lifted" (deliberately, by someone) |

## 5.6 *ter-* as "able to be ___ed" (potential)

A related reading of *ter-* expresses capability, almost always under
negation:

> Batu itu **tidak terangkat** oleh saya.
> "That rock **couldn't be lifted** by me." (I wasn't *able* to lift it.)

> Tulisannya **tidak terbaca**.
> "His/her handwriting **isn't legible** / can't be read."

## 5.7 A trap: *ter-* as superlative

*ter-* has a completely separate job when prefixed to an **adjective**
root: it forms the superlative ("-est"), with no passive meaning at all.

> *besar* (big) → **terbesar** ("biggest")
> *baik* (good) → **terbaik** ("best")
> *cantik* (pretty) → **tercantik** ("prettiest")

These are a different grammatical function sharing the same prefix shape —
don't try to force a "state/accident" reading onto *terbesar*. The
distinguishing signal is the root: adjective roots → superlative; verb
roots → passive/stative.

## 5.8 Phonological note: *ter-* + r-initial roots

Like *ber-* and *per-* (Chapters 6 and 9), *ter-* drops its *r* before a
root that itself starts with *r*, to avoid an awkward double *r*:

> *rasa* (feel) → **terasa** (not *terrasa*) — "is felt / feels [like]"

## 5.9 Choosing between *di-* and *ter-*

Ask: *is there a specific agent I'm deliberately foregrounding, or
backgrounding on purpose?* → *di-*. *Is the event accidental, agentless,
or am I describing a resulting state?* → *ter-*.

| | *di-* | *ter-* |
|---|---|---|
| Agent | Present, can be named with *oleh* | Absent / irrelevant / unintentional |
| Reading | Deliberate event | Accident, state, or potential |
| Example | *dibuka* — "was opened [by someone]" | *terbuka* — "is open" |

## Practice

Give the *di-* passive and the *ter-* form for each root, and gloss the
difference in meaning:

1. *kunci* (to lock)
2. *bakar* (to burn)
3. *lihat* (to see)

<details>
<summary>Answer key</summary>

1. *dikunci* — "was locked" (deliberate act) vs. *terkunci* — "is locked /
   got locked" (state, or accidentally locked — e.g. locked yourself out)
2. *dibakar* — "was burned" (deliberate act, e.g. burning trash) vs.
   *terbakar* — "caught fire / burned" (accidental, e.g. a house fire)
3. *dilihat* — "was seen" (deliberate act of looking, with di- passive
   object-focus) vs. *terlihat* — "visible / happened to be seen" (no
   deliberate agent foregrounded)

</details>
